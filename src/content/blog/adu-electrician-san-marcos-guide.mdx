San Marcos is booming with ADU construction. From the neighborhoods near Discovery Lake to the areas around Palomar College, homeowners are building accessory dwelling units to add value, generate rental income, or house family members. But here's what many don't realize: ADU electrical work isn't just about running wire and installing outlets. It's a specialized skill that requires deep knowledge of San Marcos building codes, utility connections, and permit processes.

Choosing the wrong electrician can delay your project by months, cost thousands in rework, or worse—result in a unit that won't pass inspection. This checklist will help you evaluate electricians in San Marcos and find someone who knows the local landscape.

## Why ADU Electrical Isn't Standard Residential Work

An ADU is technically a separate dwelling unit, which means it needs its own electrical service, separate meter, and dedicated circuits. The City of San Marcos treats ADU electrical work more like a small commercial project than a typical home renovation. Your electrician needs to understand:

- San Marcos Municipal Code Title 22 (ADU regulations)
- SDG&E service requirements and meter placement
- Panel capacity and potential upgrade needs
- Permit timelines and city inspection protocols

A general electrician familiar with standard home repairs might struggle here. You need someone who has done multiple ADU projects in San Marcos and understands the specific hoops you'll jump through.

## Checklist Item 1: Verify Licensing and Insurance

Start here—it's non-negotiable.

**What to confirm:**
- California Contractors State License Board (CSLB) Class C-10 electrical contractor license
- Current status (not expired, not under suspension)
- General liability insurance ($1 million minimum)
- Workers' compensation coverage

Go to the CSLB website and search by license number or company name. You'll see the license status, any complaints, and disciplinary history. If an electrician can't provide a license number or it doesn't check out, move on immediately.

Ask for proof of insurance in writing—not a verbal promise. Insurance protects you if someone gets hurt on your property or if electrical work damages your home.

## Checklist Item 2: Local ADU Experience

This is where many contractors fail. Ask directly:

- How many ADU projects have you completed in San Marcos?
- Can you provide references from San Marcos ADU clients?
- Have you worked with the City of San Marcos planning and building departments?
- Do you know the current SDG&E requirements for new ADU meters in our area?

An electrician with genuine San Marcos ADU experience will answer confidently and offer to connect you with past clients. They'll know that the city requires separate service, they'll understand the permit timeline, and they won't be surprised by inspection requirements.

If someone says "ADUs are just like any other renovation," that's a red flag. They're not.

## Checklist Item 3: Panel Upgrade Capability

Many San Marcos homes built in the 1970s and 1980s have 100-amp electrical panels. Adding an ADU almost always requires upgrading to 200 amps. This is complex work involving utility coordination, permit requirements, and safety compliance.

Ask your electrician:

- Have you completed electrical panel upgrades in San Marcos?
- What's your process for coordinating with SDG&E?
- How long does a typical panel upgrade take?
- What happens if my main panel can't handle the new service?

If they hesitate or seem unfamiliar with panel upgrades, that's a problem. An electrician who handles ADU work regularly will have this process down. An electrician who only does outlet repairs won't.

## Checklist Item 4: Permit and Inspection Knowledge

Permits aren't optional—they're mandatory. And they're not just paperwork. The City of San Marcos requires multiple inspections: rough-in, final electrical, and often a final building inspection. Your electrician should manage this process or at least explain it clearly.

Ask:

- Do you pull permits, or do I need to?
- What inspections will we need?
- How long does permit approval typically take in San Marcos?
- What happens if the city finds issues during inspection?

A professional electrician will handle permits as part of their service. They know the inspectors, they understand what the city looks for, and they can often expedite the process. If they tell you to pull permits yourself or seem vague about timelines, reconsider.

## Checklist Item 5: Written Estimate and Scope

Never hire based on a phone quote. Get a detailed, written estimate that includes:

- Service size (200-amp, 100-amp, etc.)
- Panel upgrade (yes/no, and cost)
- Meter and service installation
- Rough-in labor and materials
- Final wiring and fixture installation
- Permit and inspection fees
- Timeline and payment schedule

The estimate should reference San Marcos building code requirements and specify what's included and what's not. If they can't provide this level of detail, they're either inexperienced or disorganized—neither is good.

## Checklist Item 6: Communication and Professionalism

You'll be working with this person for weeks. They need to be responsive, professional, and willing to answer questions.

Notice:

- Do they return calls and emails promptly?
- Do they explain things clearly without condescension?
- Are they willing to discuss options (e.g., recessed lighting layout, outlet placement)?
- Do they provide a realistic timeline upfront?

A good electrician will be transparent about challenges. If they discover that your main panel needs replacement or that service runs are longer than expected, they'll communicate this and adjust the estimate. They won't just surprise you with a bill at the end.

## Local Context: San Marcos ADU Challenges

San Marcos has specific characteristics that affect ADU electrical work:

**Topography and soil conditions:** Parts of San Marcos have rocky or clay-heavy soil, which can complicate underground service runs. An experienced local electrician knows where these issues typically occur and can plan accordingly.

**Utility coordination:** SDG&E service in San Marcos can have variable response times depending on the area. A local electrician has relationships with SDG&E and knows how to expedite meter installation.

**Code enforcement:** The City of San Marcos building department is thorough. They inspect closely and don't accept shortcuts. An electrician who's worked here multiple times knows exactly what passes inspection on the first try.

**Neighborhood variability:** San Marcos spans from older neighborhoods near downtown to newer subdivisions near Palomar College. Older areas may have aging infrastructure that complicates new service. A local electrician understands these differences.

## Red Flags: What to Avoid

- **No verifiable license:** Non-negotiable. Period.
- **Reluctance to provide references:** If they won't name past clients, there's a reason.
- **Vague timelines:** "It'll be done when it's done" isn't acceptable.
- **Significantly lower bids than competitors:** Usually means corners will be cut.
- **No written estimate:** Verbal quotes lead to disputes.
- **Unfamiliar with San Marcos codes:** They should know the local requirements without looking them up.

## What Comes After: Inspections and Sign-Off

Once electrical work is complete, the City of San Marcos will schedule inspections. Your electrician should attend these or at least prepare you for what the inspector will check. After final approval, you'll receive a Certificate of Occupancy for your ADU—this is what allows you to legally rent it or occupy it.

A good electrician doesn't disappear after the final invoice. They're available if questions come up during inspection or if the city requests adjustments.
