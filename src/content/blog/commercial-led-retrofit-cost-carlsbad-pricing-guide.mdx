## Understanding the Cost Per Square Foot Model

The most straightforward way to estimate a commercial LED retrofit is by square footage, but the per-square-foot rate varies significantly based on what you're retrofitting.

**Basic fixture replacement** (swapping existing fluorescent or incandescent fixtures with direct LED equivalents, minimal rewiring):
- $1.50–$2.00 per sq ft
- Best for: Offices, retail spaces with standard drop-ceiling layouts
- Example: 3,000 sq ft office = $4,500–$6,000

**Retrofit with controls integration** (LED fixtures plus occupancy sensors, daylight harvesting, or dimming):
- $2.00–$2.75 per sq ft
- Best for: Open-plan offices, retail, warehouses with variable lighting needs
- Example: 5,000 sq ft showroom = $10,000–$13,750

**Full infrastructure upgrade** (new fixtures, rewiring, panel assessment, control systems, emergency lighting):
- $2.75–$3.50+ per sq ft
- Best for: Older buildings with outdated wiring, facilities needing emergency backup, complex layouts
- Example: 8,000 sq ft warehouse = $22,000–$28,000

The difference between these tiers isn't just fixture cost—it's labor, materials, and complexity. An older Carlsbad building from the 1980s with undersized circuits and corroded conduit will cost more than a 2010s-era space with modern electrical infrastructure.

## Breaking Down the Line-Item Costs

Here's what actually goes into your retrofit invoice:

**Fixtures and lamps**: 30–40% of total cost
- LED troffer fixtures: $80–$200 each
- LED high-bay fixtures: $150–$400 each
- Specialty fixtures (decorative, outdoor): $120–$500 each
- Quantity depends on your space and layout

**Labor**: 35–45% of total cost
- Fixture removal and installation
- Wiring and circuit modifications
- Testing and commissioning
- Compliance documentation

**Controls and sensors** (if included): 10–20% of total cost
- Occupancy sensors: $30–$80 each
- Daylight sensors: $60–$150 each
- Control panels and integration: $500–$2,000+

**Electrical upgrades** (if needed): 5–15% of total cost
- Panel assessment and minor rewiring
- New circuits or disconnects
- Conduit and junction box work

**Permits and compliance**: $300–$1,500
- Title 24 documentation and certification
- Local permits (Carlsbad Building Department)
- Inspection fees

## How Rebate Eligibility Affects Your Real Cost

San Diego Gas & Electric (SDG&E) offers rebates for qualifying commercial LED retrofits, but "qualifying" has specific requirements.

**Rebate eligibility checklist:**
- Retrofit must reduce energy consumption by at least 10%
- New fixtures must meet ENERGY STAR or equivalent standards
- Project must comply with California Title 24
- Lighting power density cannot exceed Title 24 limits for your space type
- Rebate application must be submitted before work begins

**Rebate amounts (typical):**
- Basic LED retrofit: 30–40% of eligible equipment and labor costs
- LED retrofit with controls: 40–50% of eligible costs
- Maximum rebate cap: Usually $50,000–$100,000 depending on project scope

**What's NOT rebatable:**
- Design fees or consulting (sometimes)
- Certain emergency lighting upgrades
- Costs above Title 24 power density limits
- Work performed without permits

For the Carlsbad research center project, the rebate was calculated on $20,250 of eligible costs (fixtures, labor, controls, and wiring). The $900 permit and documentation fee wasn't rebatable, and neither was the owner's project management time. That's why the rebate came to $8,100 rather than covering the full $21,800.

A commercial LED lighting retrofit in Carlsbad or nearby areas typically qualifies for rebates, but you need to plan for it correctly. Submit your rebate application *before* you start work. If you retrofit without pre-approval, you forfeit the rebate.

## Title 24 Compliance: What You Actually Need to Know

California Title 24 isn't a suggestion for commercial buildings—it's a legal requirement. Non-compliant installations can result in:
- Rebate denial (you lose 30–50% of your investment)
- Building permit rejection
- Fines from the California Energy Commission
- Liability if a future owner or tenant discovers the violation

**Title 24 requirements for commercial LED retrofits:**
- Lighting power density (watts per sq ft) must not exceed limits for your building type
  - Office: 0.9–1.1 W/sq ft (depending on task and space type)
  - Retail: 1.2–1.5 W/sq ft
  - Warehouse: 0.6–0.8 W/sq ft
  - Specialty spaces have their own limits
- All fixtures must be ENERGY STAR certified or equivalent
- Occupancy sensors required in certain spaces (break rooms, storage, restrooms, certain offices)
- Daylight harvesting controls required in perimeter zones with significant glazing
- Emergency lighting must meet specific standards
- All work must be documented with as-built plans and a Certificate of Compliance

What does this mean for your retrofit cost? Compliance documentation adds $300–$1,500 to your project, but it's non-negotiable. We handle all Title 24 paperwork—calculations, as-built lighting plans, sensor specifications, and the final Certificate of Compliance that goes to the building department.

Skipping this step to save money is false economy. You'll either pay for it later when you need to remediate, or lose rebates that would have covered the compliance cost several times over.

## Phased Retrofit Strategy for Larger Buildings

If you're retrofitting a 10,000+ sq ft facility or a multi-tenant building, a phased approach can spread costs and minimize operational disruption.

**Phase 1 (Months 1–2): High-use areas**
- Retrofit offices, conference rooms, and main hallways first
- Immediate energy savings and employee feedback
- Typical cost: 35–40% of total project
- Rebate application submitted after Phase 1

**Phase 2 (Months 3–4): Secondary spaces**
- Retrofit warehouses, storage, break rooms
- Coordinate with tenants or operational schedules
- Typical cost: 35–40% of total project

**Phase 3 (Months 5–6): Remaining areas and controls**
- Complete retrofit with full controls integration
- Final testing and Title 24 certification
- Typical cost: 20–30% of total project

Phased retrofits let you validate energy savings from Phase 1 before committing to the full project, and they spread cash flow over several quarters. The downside: labor costs are slightly higher because crews make multiple visits, and rebate calculations become more complex. For most buildings over 15,000 sq ft, the operational benefits outweigh the modest cost increase.

When you're ready to move forward with a commercial LED lighting retrofit, ask your contractor these questions to avoid surprises:

2. **Will you handle the SDG&E rebate application, or is that my responsibility?** A contractor who manages rebates for you eliminates delays and mistakes.
3. **What's your warranty on fixtures and labor?** Standard is 5 years on fixtures, 1 year on labor.
4. **Do you have references from similar commercial projects in Carlsbad or North County?** Local experience matters—building codes, utility practices, and contractor familiarity vary by area.
5. **Will you provide an energy savings estimate and payback calculation?** This helps justify the project to your finance team or lender.
6. **How will you minimize disruption to my business?** Off-hours work, phased schedules, and coordination with your operations team should be standard.
